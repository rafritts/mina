# Emotion and Feeling Models: Neurochemical Profiles

Neurochemical basis of 21 emotions and felt states, plus theoretical frameworks and the wanting/liking distinction.

---

## Theoretical Foundations

Before the individual models, three core principles:

### The Wanting/Liking Distinction (Berridge)

One of the most important discoveries in affective neuroscience: **wanting** and **liking** are dissociable neurobiological systems.

| System | Neurochemistry | Location | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| **WANTING** (Incentive Salience) | Mesolimbic **dopamine** (VTA → NAc, striatum, PFC) | Nucleus accumbens **core**, dorsal striatum | Makes reward-predictive stimuli attractive; generates craving and approach behavior. The motivation to pursue. |
| **LIKING** (Hedonic Impact) | Endogenous **opioids** (MOR) + endocannabinoids (CB1) | Hedonic hotspots: NAc **shell** (rostrodorsal ~10% of NAc), ventral pallidum (largest hotspot), parabrachial nucleus, OFC/insula | The actual pleasurable quality of rewarding experience. The "mmm" response. |
| **LEARNING** (Reward Prediction) | **Dopamine** (prediction error signals) | VTA, NAc, striatum | Updates value representations; Pavlovian and instrumental conditioning. Separable from both. |

**Key experimental finding**: Dopamine depletion (6-OHDA lesion) abolishes wanting — rats will not approach or work for food even if placed in the mouth. But if food is placed in the mouth, they still show liking reactions (tongue protrusions) — liking is preserved. Addicts experience intense wanting (craving) for drugs they no longer like (enjoy).

**Consequences for emotional states**:

| State | Wanting | Liking | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Normal reward | High | High | Balanced |
| Addiction (sensitized) | High (pathological) | Low/absent | High craving, low enjoyment — the trap |
| Depression/anhedonia | Low | Low | Both systems blunted |
| Hunger | High (ghrelin → VTA) | Moderate | Wanting intensified; liking returns to normal post-meal |
| Social connection | High | High | Both systems engaged |
| Loneliness | Low | Low | Both systems impaired |

### Valence and Arousal as the Raw Material

Neurochemicals generate two primary dimensions of affect that the brain then constructs into discrete emotions:

**Valence** (pleasant ↔ unpleasant):
- Pleasant: opioid/dopamine/OXT/serotonin balance dominant
- Unpleasant: cortisol/CRH/NE/substance P/glutamate-excitotoxic balance dominant

**Arousal** (high ↔ low):
- High: NE/dopamine/orexin/histamine dominant
- Low: adenosine/GABA/serotonin dominant; orexin suppressed

The brain's predictive machinery (PFC, insula, ACC) constructs **discrete emotions** by categorizing these core-affect states using learned concepts and context. The same neurochemical state (e.g., high NE + cortisol + dopamine) can be experienced as terror, excited anticipation, or awe — depending on context and available emotional concept. This is the constructionist view (Barrett, *Theory of Constructed Emotion*, 2017).

## Emotion and Feeling Models

---

### JOY / HAPPINESS

**Primary Neurochemicals**:
| Chemical | Direction | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Dopamine | ↑ (phasic) | Anticipatory pleasure, reward prediction error, motivational "wanting" |
| β-Endorphin | ↑ | "Liking" component — actual hedonic pleasure at MOR hotspots |
| Serotonin | ↑ (tonic) | Stable contentment-type happiness; mood floor; equanimity |
| Oxytocin | ↑ | Social happiness component; released during positive interactions |

**Brain Regions Most Active**:
- **VTA**: Dopamine origin; phasic firing at unexpected positive outcomes
- **Nucleus accumbens / ventral striatum**: Core reward and motivational value encoding
- **OFC**: Subjective value ("liking"); essential for experienced pleasure
- **Left DLPFC**: Greater left-than-right prefrontal activation = positive affect (Davidson's approach/withdrawal asymmetry)
- **ACC**: Reward appraisal; integrates emotional and cognitive processing
- **Amygdala**: Reduced threat activation; possible positive valence processing
- **Insula**: Interoceptive awareness of positive bodily states

**Time Course**:
- Phasic dopamine burst: milliseconds to seconds at reward delivery
- Opioid-mediated liking: seconds to minutes
- Serotonin-based contentment: tonic, sustained hours to days

**Transitions**:
- Sustained joy → contentment (dopamine normalizes; serotonin stays elevated; cortisol low)
- Loss of reward → disappointment (dopamine dip = negative prediction error)
- Joy + arousal → excitement (NE co-elevation)

**Habituation**: Dopamine prediction error shrinks with repetition — reward becomes "expected." Requires novelty or uncertainty to restore. Chronic D2 downregulation → anhedonia and emotional blunting.

### FEAR

**Primary Neurochemicals**:
| Chemical | Direction | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Norepinephrine | ↑ (phasic, from LC) | Autonomic arousal, heightened vigilance; potentiates fear memory consolidation via β-adrenergic in lateral amygdala |
| CRH | ↑ (hypothalamus and CeA) | Activates HPA; acts as neuromodulator in amygdala and BNST; CRHR1 mediates acute anxiety/fear |
| Cortisol | ↑ (15–30 min post-stressor) | Permissive role — facilitates NE effects on amygdala for optimal fear memory formation |
| Glutamate (NMDA) | ↑ | Encodes fear memories in lateral amygdala; coincidence detection for CS-US associations |
| GABA | Inhibitory check | Intercalated cell masses (ITCs) and interneurons gate amygdala output; low GABA worsens fear |

**Brain Regions Most Active**:
- **Lateral amygdala (LA)**: Input stage; encodes CS-US associations via glutamate LTP
- **Basolateral amygdala (BLA)**: Integrates threat associations; projects to CeA
- **Central amygdala (CeA)**: Output nucleus; drives freezing behavior via brainstem; contains CRH neurons
- **BNST**: Mediates sustained/uncertain anxiety (vs. phasic fear in CeA; CRH and CRHR2 dense)
- **Hippocampus**: Contextual fear conditioning
- **PAG**: Defensive behaviors (freezing, flight, analgesia)
- **vmPFC**: Inhibits CeA during extinction; extinction memory stored here
- **LC**: NE source projecting to amygdala, cortex, hippocampus

**Time Course**:
- Phasic fear: seconds (LA → CeA pathway)
- Sustained anxiety: minutes to hours (BNST and CRH)
- HPA cortisol response: peaks ~20–30 min; returns to baseline ~60–90 min
- Fear memory consolidation: hours to days (NE + cortisol window critical within ~6 h post-encoding)
- Extinction: new inhibitory learning (vmPFC-based); competes with original fear trace

**Transitions**:
- Phasic fear → anxiety (CeA → BNST shift; concrete threat → diffuse uncertainty)
- Repeated fear → PTSD (vmPFC extinction fails; amygdala hyperactive; cortisol dysregulation)
- Fear + reward context → excitement/thrill (NE + dopamine co-elevation)

**Habituation**: Repeated non-reinforced CS → extinction (new vmPFC/IL cortex memory). NMDA blockade during extinction impairs extinction learning. Context-specific: fear spontaneously recovers when context changes (mechanism of PTSD persistence in triggering environments).

### ANGER / RAGE

**Primary Neurochemicals**:
| Chemical | Direction | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Norepinephrine | ↑ | Physical arousal; heart rate/BP; lowers aggression threshold (works with testosterone) |
| Testosterone | ↑ (facilitating) | Lowers amygdala-PFC inhibitory threshold; permissive, not directly triggering |
| Serotonin | ↓ | Low 5-HT impairs PFC regulation of amygdala — the "brake" fails; 5-HTTLPR short allele carriers show stronger amygdala response to angry faces |
| Cortisol | ↑ (acute) | Chronic elevations associated with irritability and reactive aggression |
| Vasopressin | ↑ | V1a receptor in amygdala and lateral septum promotes defensive/territorial aggression |

**Brain Regions Most Active**:
- **Amygdala (CeA)**: Affective core of anger; triggers autonomic response
- **Hypothalamus**: Coordinates physiological aggression response (vocalization, autonomic)
- **vmPFC / OFC**: Suppresses inappropriate aggression; impaired → impulsive aggression
- **ACC**: Conflict monitoring; engaged when inhibiting aggressive impulses
- **NAc**: Dopamine-driven frustration/reward-blocking response
- **Insula**: Interoceptive "heat" of anger

**Time Course**:
- Acute anger: seconds to minutes (NE surge, HPA pre-activation)
- Sustained rage: minutes to hours (cortisol elevation, reduced serotonin tone)
- Post-anger fatigue: NE and cortisol normalize; possible serotonin "rebound"

**Key Receptors**: 5-HT2A (postsynaptic PFC — reduced in impulsive aggression); 5-HT1B (terminal autoreceptors — knockout mice show increased aggression); V1a vasopressin (lateral septum, amygdala — territorial aggression).

### SADNESS / GRIEF (Situational)

**Primary Neurochemicals**:
| Chemical | Direction | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Serotonin | ↓ (tonic) | Reduced mood floor, hedonic capacity, impulse regulation; anhedonia |
| Dopamine | ↓ (especially tonic) | Anhedonia, amotivation, loss of interest in pleasurable activities |
| CRH | ↑ | Elevated hypothalamic CRH mimics depressive symptoms (appetite ↓, sleep disrupted, drive ↓) |
| Cortisol | ↑ | HPA hyperactivity characteristic of major depression |
| Endogenous opioids | Disrupted | Loss events disrupt opioid system homeostasis; "social opioid withdrawal" state |
| Norepinephrine | ↓ (vegetative depression) | Low NE contributes to psychomotor slowing, fatigue, concentration deficits |

**Brain Regions Most Active**:
- **sgACC (subgenual ACC, BA25)**: Hyperactive in depression; key DBS target for treatment-resistant depression; modulates HPA, autonomic tone, emotional reactivity
- **Amygdala**: Hyperactive; biased toward negative stimuli
- **Hippocampus**: Reduced volume in chronic depression (cortisol-mediated glucocorticoid neurotoxicity)
- **Anterior insula**: Interoceptive sadness; "heaviness"
- **dACC**: Activated during social pain/loss
- **DLPFC**: Hypoactive — impairs cognitive control of negative affect

**Time Course**:
- Acute sadness: hours (dopamine and opioid dip)
- Grief: days to weeks (opioid dysregulation gradually normalizing)
- Prolonged grief / MDD: months to years (stable neurobiological changes; hippocampal atrophy)

**Habituation**: Monoamine depletion creates a vicious cycle (low 5-HT → 5-HT1A desensitization failure → even lower serotonin output). With successful grief processing: gradual opioid tone restoration, HPA normalization, dopamine recovery.

### DISGUST

**Primary Neurochemicals**:
- **Serotonin** (modulatory): serotonergic tone modulates disgust sensitivity
- **GABA/glutamate balance (basal ganglia)**: hyperactive cortico-striatal-thalamo-cortical circuits (OCD model) amplify disgust

**Brain Regions Most Active**:
- **Anterior insula** (primary): bilateral activation for contamination, mutilation, rotten food stimuli, and observing others' disgust. Electrical stimulation of left anterior insula can directly elicit disgust experience.
- **Basal ganglia / putamen**: visceral disgust; hyperactive in OCD contamination
- **OFC**: Evaluates aversive taste/smell
- **ACC**: Affective evaluation

**Neuroimaging**: Wicker et al. (2003, *Neuron*): same insula voxels activated by feeling disgust AND observing others' disgust — basis for emotional contagion.

### SURPRISE

**Primary Neurochemicals**:
| Chemical | Direction | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Norepinephrine | ↑ (phasic, from LC) | Orienting response; synchronous response to unexpected stimuli regardless of valence; promotes sensorimotor plasticity |
| Dopamine | ↑ or pause | Positive surprise → phasic DA burst (positive prediction error); negative surprise → dopamine pause (negative PE) |
| Acetylcholine | ↑ (phasic, basal forebrain) | Signals uncertainty; modulates attentional reorienting |

**Brain Regions**: VTA + SNc (dopamine PE), LC (NE orienting), NAc (PE-updated value representations), OFC/vmPFC (value prediction), ACC (surprise/conflict detection), superior colliculus (orienting reflex).

**Time Course**:
- NE phasic burst: within 50–150 ms of surprising stimulus
- Dopamine PE: 200–400 ms latency
- Cortical reappraisal: 500+ ms

**Transitions**:
- Positive surprise → excitement/joy (dopamine + NE → reward anticipation)
- Negative surprise → fear/anxiety (NE + CRH cascade)
- Neutral surprise → curiosity (dopamine + ACh)

### LOVE / ATTACHMENT (Romantic)

**Early-stage (Lust/Infatuation)**:
| Chemical | Direction | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Dopamine | ↑↑ | Caudate/VTA activation — the "addiction" circuitry; obsessive craving quality |
| Norepinephrine | ↑ | Energy, sleeplessness, focused attention on partner |
| Serotonin | ↓ (~40% reduction) | Resembles OCD; drives obsessive thinking. Newly-in-love individuals match OCD patients in serotonin levels |
| Cortisol | ↑ (mildly) | Stress-like state of early attraction |

**Long-term attachment**:
| Chemical | Direction | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Oxytocin | ↑ | Released during touch, sex, childbirth; promotes pair bonding, trust, mate-guarding |
| Vasopressin | ↑ (V1a) | Strong pair-bonding agent; promotes monogamy-like behavior; social memory and mate defense |
| β-Endorphin / opioids | ↑ (tonic) | Long-term attachment generates tonic opioid release from social proximity — the "comfort" of a close partner |
| Dopamine | ↑ (sustained in happy long-term couples) | Neuroimaging: 20+ year happy couples maintain VTA dopamine activity |

**Brain Regions Most Active**:
- **Caudate nucleus** (early love): reward expectation and goal-directed behavior
- **VTA**: dopamine generation
- **NAc / ventral striatum**: reward and bonding reinforcement
- **PVN**: oxytocin synthesis
- **Medial amygdala**: oxytocin-mediated social recognition
- **Lateral habenula** (suppressed): reduced negative evaluations of partner ("rose-colored glasses")
- **PFC** (partially deactivated, early love): "love is blind" — critical judgment PFC quiets

**Time Course**:
- Infatuation: weeks to months (high dopamine, low serotonin)
- Transition (6–18 months): serotonin normalizes; oxytocin/vasopressin matures
- Secure attachment (years): tonic opioid and oxytocin system dominant; dopamine reinforces positive interactions

**Transitions**:
- Early love → secure attachment (dopamine ↓; opioid/oxytocin ↑)
- Rejection → social pain (opioid withdrawal-like; dACC/insula activation mimicking physical pain)
- Loss of attachment → grief (opioid disruption; CRH elevation)

**Habituation**: Dopamine habituates to familiar partner (prediction error shrinks). Explains fading of intense early passion. Oxytocin may downregulate with chronic exposure, but opioid system for social comfort is resilient.

### ROMANTIC DESIRE

**Primary Neurochemicals**:
| Chemical | Direction | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Testosterone | ↑ | Primary drive driver (both sexes; via conversion to estradiol in brain); acts on hypothalamic MPOA |
| Estrogen | ↑ (facilitating, females) | Sensitizes MPOA; promotes progesterone receptor expression |
| Dopamine | ↑ | Incentive salience for romantic/romantic stimuli; approach and arousal; MPOA dopamine critical for male motivated approach |
| Nitric oxide (NO) | ↑ | Peripheral vasodilator essential for physiological arousal (arousal response) via eNOS |
| Oxytocin / vasopressin | ↑ at peak bonding | Post-peak pair bonding; spike at peak bonding |
| Prolactin | ↑ post-peak | Refractory period and satiety |

**Brain Regions**: MPOA of hypothalamus (primary romantic motivation center; high testosterone receptors), VMH (female receptive bonding behavior), NAc (dopamine approach motivation), VTA, amygdala, anterior insula, OFC, PVN (oxytocin at peak resolution).

**Time Course**: Desire (minutes to hours; testosterone-sensitized dopamine/NE) → Arousal (minutes; NO-mediated peripheral vasodilation; dopamine/NE peak) → Peak (seconds; oxytocin surge, endorphin burst) → Resolution (prolactin; refractory period).

**Habituation — Coolidge Effect**: Dopamine response to familiar romantic partner habituates; novel partner restores dopaminergic motivation (well-established in animal models).

### LONELINESS

**Primary Neurochemicals**:
| Chemical | Direction | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Endogenous opioids | ↓ / disrupted | "Social opioid withdrawal": social connection generates tonic MOR stimulation; loneliness = insufficient MOR activation. Opioid agonists reduce separation distress across mammals; naltrexone worsens social comfort. |
| CRH | ↑ | Acute social isolation → CRH from CeA and hypothalamus → HPA activation |
| Cortisol | ↑ (chronic) | Chronic loneliness associated with elevated cortisol awakening response (CAR) |
| Dopamine | ↓ (blunted) | Reduced mesolimbic dopamine tone in chronic loneliness → anhedonia and social withdrawal |
| Oxytocin | ↓ (baseline) | Chronic loneliness → reduced oxytocin tone → impaired social reward processing |
| Substance P | ↑ (implied) | Dorsal striatum co-release; social pain and aversive emotional states |

**Brain Regions Most Active**:
- **dACC**: Both physical and social pain activated here — exclusion activates same regions as physical hurt (Cyberball paradigm fMRI)
- **Anterior insula**: Interoceptive social pain
- **Amygdala**: Hypervigilant threat detection (lonely individuals show heightened amygdala response to social threats)
- **Ventral striatum**: Reduced reward response to social stimuli (dopamine blunting)
- **PAG**: Opioid-mediated social pain suppression circuit (under-activated)

**Time Course**:
- Acute: hours (NE + CRH spike; opioid dip; hypervigilant scanning)
- Subacute: days to weeks (cortisol CAR elevated; sleep disruption; immune changes)
- Chronic: months to years (structural changes; persistent dopamine blunting; amygdala hyperreactivity; reduced oxytocin tone → vicious cycle)

**Transitions**:
- Loneliness → depression (when dopamine blunting + opioid disruption + CRH/cortisol exceed adaptive capacity)
- Loneliness → addiction vulnerability (seeking opioid drugs to substitute for absent social bonding opioid stimulation)
- Social reconnection → acute opioid and oxytocin restoration → relief

**Habituation**: Chronic loneliness → amygdala sensitization (increases hypervigilance and threat-detection bias, *decreasing* safe social approach over time — a self-reinforcing spiral).

### ANXIETY

**Primary Neurochemicals**:
| Chemical | Direction | Role |
|---|---|---|
| GABA | ↓ | Primary inhibitory brake; reduced GABAergic tone → overexcited neurons → anxiety |
| CRH | ↑ (hypothalamus, CeA, BNST) | Both HPA activator and direct amygdala neuromodulator; CRHR1 mediates acute anxiety |
| Norepinephrine | ↑ (from LC) | Racing heart, sweaty palms, hyperalertness, muscle tension — the somatic anxiety experience |
| Cortisol | ↑ (chronic via HPA) | Feeds back to enhance amygdala reactivity; impairs hippocampal memory consolidation |
| Glutamate | ↑ (in some subtypes) | Excitatory excess; frontal cortex glutamate ~13% higher in social anxiety (MRS studies); NMDA in PVN contributes to HPA hyperactivity |

**Brain Regions Most Active**:
- **Amygdala (CeA and BLA)**: Core anxiety generator; hyperactive in all anxiety disorders
- **BNST**: Sustained/anticipatory anxiety (uncertain threat); distinct from acute CeA-mediated fear; CRH-dense
- **Hippocampus**: Contextual threat memory; volume reduced in chronic anxiety/PTSD
- **Anterior insula**: Interoceptive anxiety (bodily arousal awareness)
- **dACC**: Threat detection and response selection
- **PFC** (hypoactive): Impaired top-down amygdala regulation
- **LC**: NE origin; hyperactive in panic disorder
- **PVN**: CRH release; HPA activation

**Time Course**:
- Acute panic: seconds to minutes (NE burst, amygdala activation, autonomic surge)
- Sustained anxiety: hours (CRH-BNST mediated)
- Chronic (GAD): months to years (HPA dysregulation; structural amygdala changes; hippocampal atrophy)

**Transitions**:
- Anxiety → fear (threat becomes concrete: CeA takes over from BNST)
- Anxiety → calm (GABA enhancement, cortisol normalization, NE returns to tonic baseline)
- Chronic anxiety → depression (opioid depletion, dopamine blunting, persistent CRH elevation)

**Habituation**: Benzodiazepine tolerance (GABA-A downregulation → rebound anxiety on withdrawal). Cortisol receptor desensitization → impaired HPA feedback → cortisol chronically elevated. Behavioral tolerance via extinction learning (vmPFC activation = new inhibitory memory, not erasure).

### EXCITEMENT / ANTICIPATION

**Primary Neurochemicals**:
| Chemical | Direction | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Dopamine | ↑↑ ("wanting") | Dopamine neurons fire *before* reward delivery, not at delivery — dopamine IS anticipation. Progressive ramp-up as expected reward approaches. |
| Norepinephrine | ↑ | Arousal, heightened attention, uncertainty signaling |
| Orexin | ↑ | LH orexin neurons activated by rewarding events; project to VTA; drive motivated anticipatory arousal |
| Acetylcholine | ↑ (phasic) | Attentional focus on anticipated reward |

**Brain Regions**: NAc core (dopamine ramp signal encoding proximity to reward), VTA (ramp activity), lateral hypothalamus (orexin cell bodies), OFC (reward value prediction), DLPFC (temporal planning), ACC (action selection), LC (NE arousal).

**Note**: fMRI often shows the NAc BOLD signal is *higher during anticipation* (cue period) than at actual reward delivery — consistent with dopamine being fundamentally an anticipation/wanting signal.

**Transitions**:
- Anticipation fulfilled → joy (opioid liking + dopamine resolution)
- Anticipation frustrated → anger or sadness (dopamine dip below baseline)
- Prolonged anticipation → anxiety (NE + CRH elements emerge)

### CALM / CONTENTMENT

**Primary Neurochemicals**:
| Chemical | Direction | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Serotonin | ↑ (tonic) | Stable emotional floor; reduces reactivity; promotes patience and behavioral inhibition |
| GABA | ↑ (functioning tone) | Inhibitory balance; reduces neural noise; relaxation and safety signal |
| Oxytocin | ↑ (moderate) | Reduces amygdala reactivity; promotes parasympathetic tone |
| Cortisol | ↓ | Calm is partly defined by HPA quiescence (diurnal nadir correlates with calm/relaxed states) |
| β-Endorphin | ↑ (moderate tonic) | "All is well" signal from social safety; opioid-mediated contentment |
| Norepinephrine | Low tonic | Resting LC firing ~1–2 Hz = calm alertness (above = anxiety; below = drowsiness) |

**Brain Regions**: vmPFC/mPFC (amygdala suppression), raphe nuclei (serotonin output), anterior insula (low activation), PVN (low CRH; oxytocin maintenance release), NAc (moderate background dopamine tone).

**Transitions**: Calm + novelty → excitement. Calm + threat → anxiety (GABA-NE-CRH disruption). Calm → drowsiness (adenosine accumulates, orexin fades, NE tonic level drops).

### HUNGER

**Primary Neurochemicals**:
| Chemical | Direction | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Ghrelin | ↑ | Primary hunger hormone; rises before meals; activates arcuate NPY/AgRP neurons; activates VTA dopamine → shifts reward toward food cues |
| Orexin | ↑ | Bridges hunger and arousal; activates feeding circuits and enhances arousal to pursue food |
| NPY / AgRP | ↑ (arcuate) | Most potent central appetite stimulant; NPY via Y1/Y5; AgRP antagonizes MC4R to disinhibit feeding |
| Leptin | ↓ | Falling leptin removes tonic inhibition of NPY/AgRP neurons → hunger increases |
| Dopamine | ↑ (shifted toward food cues) | Ghrelin → VTA dopamine → enhanced NAc dopamine response to food-predictive cues |

**Brain Regions**: Arcuate nucleus (primary hunger signal integration; NPY/AgRP vs. POMC balance), lateral hypothalamus (orexin; the "hunger drive" and arousal), NTS (vagal inputs from GI), VTA/NAc (ghrelin-activated dopamine for food reward), OFC (food cue valuation), hippocampus (context memory for eating occasions), insula (interoceptive hunger: stomach growling, distension).

**Time Course**: Ghrelin peaks ~30–60 min before meal time (circadian-entrained). NPY/AgRP: sustained during energy deficit. Post-meal: ghrelin falls within 30–60 min.

**Transitions**: Hunger + smell/sight of food → craving (dopamine + ghrelin acute amplification). Food consumption → satiety cascade (CCK, GLP-1, leptin — see Satiety). Extreme hunger → irritability (hypoglycemia → cortisol/NE release; serotonin synthesis requires tryptophan access via glucose competition).

**Habituation — Leptin Resistance**: Chronic overconsumption and high adiposity → LepRb downregulation → chronic hunger despite adequate energy stores.

### SATIETY / FULLNESS

**Primary Neurochemicals**:
| Chemical | Direction | Role |
|---|---|---|
| CCK (cholecystokinin) | ↑ (from duodenum) | Released within minutes of fat/protein entering small intestine; activates vagal CCK-A receptors → NTS → hypothalamus; signals meal termination |
| Leptin | ↑ (rising post-meal, sustained) | Inhibits NPY/AgRP neurons in ARC; activates POMC/CART neurons; reduces dopamine in NAc |
| GLP-1 | ↑ (from L-cells of intestine) | Acts on GLP-1R in hypothalamus, hindbrain, and mesolimbic areas; GLP-1R agonists (semaglutide) reduce food-seeking by suppressing mesolimbic dopamine — mechanism of dramatic weight loss |
| PYY3-36 | ↑ (from distal gut) | Y2 receptor agonist; inhibits NPY/AgRP neurons |
| Serotonin (5-HT3 in gut) | ↑ | Enteroendocrine cells release 5-HT on nutrient stimulation → activates 5-HT3 on vagal afferents → NTS → satiety signal |
| Dopamine | Normalizes | As hunger-driven dopamine elevation resolves, reward salience of food cues diminishes |

**Brain Regions**: NTS (vagal convergence of CCK/GLP-1/stomach distension), ARC POMC/CART neurons (leptin-activated), PVN (satiety integration output), NAc (reduced dopamine), OFC (reduced food cue value post-meal), insula (interoceptive fullness).

**Time Course**: CCK (minutes; rapid satiation). GLP-1/PYY (15–30 min; sustained 2–3 h). Leptin (hours to days; cumulative energy stores). Return to hunger: CCK cleared ~90 min; ghrelin begins rising 2–4 h post-meal.

### FATIGUE / EXHAUSTION

**Primary Neurochemicals**:
| Chemical | Direction | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Adenosine | ↑ (accumulating) | Primary "sleep pressure" molecule; accumulates proportional to neural/metabolic activity; A1 receptors inhibit wake-promoting neurons (BF cholinergic, orexin); A2A in striatum promotes sleep |
| Orexin | ↓ (relative suppression) | Adenosine A1 receptors inhibit LH orexin neurons; normally orexin maintains wakefulness via NE/5-HT/histamine |
| Norepinephrine | ↓ (tonic) | Declines with sustained wakefulness; underlies mental fog of exhaustion |
| Dopamine | ↓ | Prolonged exertion depletes dopamine; reduced striatal dopaminergic activity → amotivation, reduced performance |
| Histamine | ↓ (falling) | Tuberomammillary nucleus histamine is suppressed during fatigue/sleep pressure |

**Brain Regions**: Basal forebrain / VLPO (ventrolateral preoptic area — activates with fatigue; GABAergic VLPO inhibits wake-promoting systems), lateral hypothalamus (orexin suppression), LC (reduced NE), raphe (reduced 5-HT), striatum (reduced dopamine → reduced motor/motivational drive), PFC (most vulnerable to sleep deprivation — executive function impaired earliest).

**Time Course**: Adenosine accumulation is linear across waking hours; dissipates during NREM sleep. Cognitive fatigue occurs faster than physical fatigue for equivalent load.

**Transitions**: Fatigue → sleep (adenosine overwhelms orexin/wake systems; VLPO GABAergic inhibition of LC, raphe, TMN dominates). Fatigue + forced wakefulness → anxiety/irritability (cortisol rises under sleep deprivation; emotional regulation impaired).

### FLOW STATE

**Primary Neurochemicals**:
| Chemical | Direction | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Dopamine | ↑ | Reward for challenge mastery; pattern recognition enhancement; motivational fuel |
| Norepinephrine | ↑ (optimal) | Hones focus; sensory processing efficiency; arousal without anxiety signature (LC at optimal tonic-phasic balance) |
| Anandamide | ↑ | Promotes lateral thinking and creative pattern recognition; anti-anxiety; the "timeless" quality; dampens excessive LC stress reactivity |
| Serotonin | ↑ (post-flow) | Emerges in the post-flow "afterglow" — satisfied contentment following a session |
| Cortisol | ↓ | Low stress response despite high challenge (skill-challenge balance prevents threat appraisal) |

**Brain Regions**: PFC (transient hypofrontality — DLPFC partially deactivates; reduces self-consciousness and time awareness), VTA/NAc (dopamine reward for skill execution), ACC (monitoring/updating performance), cerebellum (automated procedural execution; allows conscious PFC to disengage), motor cortex and sensory cortices (highly active in skill execution).

**Transient Hypofrontality Hypothesis**: During deep flow, the self-critical, self-monitoring DLPFC partially deactivates. This is why flow reduces self-consciousness, reduces time awareness, and produces the effortless quality despite high performance.

**Time Course**: Entry into flow ~10–20 min of sustained engagement (NE + dopamine priming). Deep flow: minutes to hours. Exit: sudden awareness of disengagement; serotonin afterglow follows.

**Transitions**: Flow interrupted → frustration (NE without dopamine reward). Flow deprived → boredom (insufficient dopamine). Post-flow → calm contentment.

### GRIEF / LOSS (Prolonged)

**Primary Neurochemicals**:
| Chemical | Direction | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Endogenous opioids | ↓ / disrupted | Defining signature of grief. Attachment figures are tied to tonic MOR stimulation; their loss creates "social opioid withdrawal." Separation distress mimics opioid withdrawal in animal models. |
| CRH | ↑ | CRH from CeA and hypothalamus drives stress response of loss |
| Cortisol | ↑↑ (acute spike) then dysregulation | Acute bereavement: cortisol surges; complicated grief: HPA dysregulation |
| Dopamine | ↑ (craving/longing) | fMRI of complicated grief (prolonged grief disorder, PGD): NAc/ventral striatum activation when viewing images of the deceased — the **craving signal**. In normal grief, pain networks dominate; in PGD, reward craving persists abnormally. |
| Serotonin | ↓ | Contributes to depressive quality of sustained grief |

**Brain Regions**:
- **dACC**: Activated in normal grief and PGD; the social pain region; responds similarly to physical pain
- **Anterior insula**: Pain and interoceptive distress
- **NAc / ventral striatum**: Abnormally persistent reward/craving activation in PGD — the longing circuit
- **Amygdala**: Emotional reactivity to reminders of deceased
- **Hippocampus**: Autobiographical memory retrieval of the deceased

**Time Course**:
- Acute grief: hours to days (cortisol spike, NE elevation, opioid dip)
- Normal grief trajectory: weeks to months (gradual opioid normalization, HPA recovery, reward craving for deceased diminishes)
- **Prolonged grief disorder (PGD)**: Months to years — NAc reward craving for deceased remains active. Naltrexone studies suggest opioid receptor manipulation can dramatically alter PGD symptoms within days.

### PRIDE / SOCIAL REWARD

**Primary Neurochemicals**:
| Chemical | Direction | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Dopamine | ↑ (ventral striatum) | Social achievement activates same reward circuitry as material rewards; VTA → NAc |
| Serotonin | ↑ (status-linked) | Serotonergic system tracks social dominance; dominant individuals have higher serotonin tone; tryptophan depletion reduces dominant behavior |
| Testosterone | ↑ (post-victory) | "Winner effect" — competitive encounters elevate testosterone and serotonin; positive feedback reinforcing dominant positioning |
| β-Endorphin | ↑ | Social acknowledgment ("tribal approval opioid hit") — being recognized by the group provides opioid reward |

**Brain Regions**: Ventral striatum/NAc (primary social success reward), VTA (dopamine), vmPFC (positive self-appraisal), dorsal raphe (serotonin/status processing; projects to ventral striatum and mPFC), ACC (self-evaluation and social comparison).

---

### SHAME / GUILT

**Primary Neurochemicals**:
| Chemical | Direction | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Cortisol | ↑↑ | Shame is the most physiologically stressful self-conscious emotion; recalling shame → significantly higher cortisol than guilt or neutral memories; reduced vagal tone |
| Norepinephrine | ↑ | Shame activates flight-like response (wanting to hide/escape via amygdala/autonomic activation) |
| Serotonin | ↓ | Social defeat and shame reduce serotonin tone |
| Dopamine | ↓ (ventral striatum) | Social defeat and shame — opposite of social reward |

**Guilt vs. Shame (neurobiological)**:
- **Guilt**: Specific, behavior-focused. Activates paracingulate dmPFC and vmPFC. Less cortisol elevation. Motivates repair behavior.
- **Shame**: Global self-condemnation. Broad mPFC self-referential networks; higher cortisol; more likely to lead to withdrawal.

**Brain Regions**: mPFC (paracingulate/dmPFC — "I am bad" representation), vmPFC (guilt-specific; sgACC overlap), amygdala (threat/social rejection), anterior insula ("sick feeling" of being exposed), ACC (subgenual), TPJ (theory of mind — imagining how others see you).

---

### TRUST

**Primary Neurochemicals**:
| Chemical | Direction | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Oxytocin | ↑ | Kosfeld et al. (2005): intranasal OXT increases trust in economic games; reduces amygdala reactivity to social threat; promotes social approach |
| Serotonin | ↑ | Promotes prosocial behavior, patience, tolerance of uncertainty — prerequisites for extending trust |
| Cortisol | ↓ | Trust requires felt safety; elevated cortisol = threat state = reduced trust; OXT directly attenuates cortisol responses to social stress |
| Dopamine | ↑ (reward anticipation) | Positive trust experiences → prediction-error dopamine → reinforces trust behavior via VTA → NAc |

**Brain Regions**: Amygdala (reduced activation — OXT suppresses threat reactivity), vmPFC (social value and safety signal), NAc (trust reward), PVN (OXT synthesis), insula (reduced negative interoception).

---

### AWE / WONDER

**Primary Neurochemicals**:
| Chemical | Direction | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Norepinephrine | ↑ (phasic) | Novelty/alert signal; orientation response to something vastly larger/more complex than existing schemas |
| Dopamine | ↑ | "This matters, learn it" signal; awe contains a prediction-error component (world is bigger than expected) |
| Oxytocin | ↑ | Promotes prosocial feelings and social connectedness; the "softening" of the moment after the electric initial awe |
| Anandamide | ↑ (possible) | The timeless, expansive quality; endocannabinoid-mediated temporal/spatial processing expansion (similar to flow and psychedelic states) |

**Brain Regions**:
- **Default Mode Network (DMN) — reduced**: Awe associated with reduced activity in precuneus and other DMN hubs. The "small self" phenomenon — self becomes smaller relative to the vast stimulus. Greater awe intensity = greater precuneus deactivation.
- **Anterior insula**: Interoceptive awe (chills, goosebumps)
- **ACC**: Appraisal of vastness and novelty
- **VTA/NAc**: Dopaminergic reward for meaningful novelty

**Transitions**: Awe → wonder (sustained curiosity — dopamine/NE maintained). Awe → transcendence (maximum DMN suppression; overlap with psychedelic states). Awe → fear (if vast stimulus is threatening — NE → CRH cascade).

## Receptor Sensitivity and Emotional Experience Over Time

### Downregulation (Desensitization)
Chronic overstimulation → receptor downregulation:
1. G-protein uncoupling (seconds)
2. GRK phosphorylation + β-arrestin binding (minutes)
3. Receptor internalization (minutes to hours)
4. Lysosomal degradation (hours)

Emotional consequence: same amount of neurochemical → less effect. The emotional "ceiling" drops.

### Upregulation (Sensitization)
Chronic understimulation → receptor upregulation → supersensitivity. Smaller stimulus → larger effect.

### Key System Examples

| System | Chronic State | Receptor Change | Emotional Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Serotonin / SSRIs** | Chronic SSRI → 5-HT floods synapses → 5-HT1A autoreceptors activated → raphe firing initially suppressed | After 2–4 weeks: 5-HT1A autoreceptors desensitize → raphe firing resumes at higher tonic level | Therapeutic antidepressant effect. SSRIs take weeks because autoreceptor desensitization is the rate-limiting step. |
| **Dopamine D2** | Chronic D2 agonism / drugs | D2 selectively degraded (vs. D1 which recycles) → D2 downregulation | Anhedonia — cannot register reward; addictive cycle |
| **HPA / Cortisol** | Chronic stress | GR downregulation in hippocampus/PFC → impaired HPA feedback | Cortisol chronically elevated; even mild stressors feel catastrophic; PFC regulatory capacity reduced; amygdala hyperreactivity |
| **Oxytocin (OXTR)** | Early life adversity | Persistent OXTR downregulation | Reduced ability to benefit from social support as adult |
| **Opioids (MOR)** | Chronic opioid drug use | Dramatic MOR downregulation | Social pleasure impaired; physical pain hypersensitivity; persistent anhedonia during abstinence |
| **GABA-A / Benzodiazepines** | Chronic BZ use | α1 and α2 subunit redistribution/reduction | Tolerance to sedation and anxiolysis; rebound hyperexcitability, panic, seizure risk on withdrawal |

### The General Setpoint Principle
Every neurochemical system has a homeostatic setpoint. Emotional experience reflects the **ratio of current neurochemical level to the adapted setpoint**, not the absolute level. This is why:
- Chronically depressed individuals experience their neurochemical baseline as "normal"
- Post-addiction individuals find natural rewards inadequate (setpoint recalibrated for drug-level dopamine)
- After sustained meditation or exercise, emotional volatility decreases (setpoints normalize, receptor sensitivities stabilize)
